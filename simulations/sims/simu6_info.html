Predator-Prey Ecosystem

Spatial Lotka-Volterra dynamics with day/night cycles and safe zones

What is Predator-Prey Dynamics?

The predator-prey relationship is one of the most fundamental interactions in ecology. First modeled mathematically by Alfred Lotka (1925) and Vito Volterra (1926), these equations describe how predator and prey populations oscillate in a characteristic pattern: prey boom → predator boom → prey crash → predator crash → repeat.

This simulation brings these dynamics to life with spatially explicit agents — rabbits and foxes — that move, eat, reproduce, and die in a 2D landscape with grass, burrows, and day/night cycles.

Ecology Behind the Simulation

This simulation models a complete ecosystem with multiple interacting mechanisms:

  • Rabbits: Seek grass in the growing area for food. Flee to burrows (safe zones) when foxes are detected within 150 pixels. Die of starvation after 2 days without food. Reproduce every 15 days if population is below carrying capacity.
  • Foxes: Hunt rabbits within their vision range. Die of starvation after 4 days without catching prey. Reproduce every 25 days if recently fed and population is below carrying capacity.
  • Day/Night Cycle: Foxes are more effective hunters at night, adding temporal complexity to the predator-prey dynamics.
  • Spatial Refugia: Burrows act as safe zones where rabbits cannot be caught, stabilizing the ecosystem by preventing total prey extinction.
  • Food Regeneration: Grass regrows naturally in designated areas, providing the base resource that supports the entire food chain.

Key Equations

The classical Lotka-Volterra equations that this simulation approximates:

dR/dt = αR - βRF    (prey growth - predation)
dF/dt = δRF - γF    (predation benefit - predator death)

Where R = rabbit population, F = fox population, α = prey growth rate, β = predation rate, δ = predator efficiency, γ = predator death rate.

Unlike the classical model, this simulation adds spatial structure, stochasticity, and environmental features that create richer, more realistic dynamics.

Simulation Controls

  • Initial Rabbits / Foxes: Starting populations (5-50 rabbits, 1-20 foxes).
  • Day Length: Duration of each day/night cycle (5-30 seconds).
  • Food Growth Rate: Grass regeneration speed (0.5-3.0). Affects prey carrying capacity.
  • Rabbit / Fox Speed: Movement velocities. Faster foxes catch more prey but also burn energy faster.
  • Fox Vision Range: Hunting detection radius (50-300 pixels). Larger range = more effective predators.
  • Reproduction Periods: Gestation intervals for rabbits (10-25 days) and foxes (15-40 days).

Interesting Behaviors to Observe

  • Population Oscillations: Watch the live population graph to see classic predator-prey cycles with the predator peak lagging behind the prey peak.
  • Boom-Bust Cycles: Rapid prey growth followed by predator population explosion, prey collapse, and then predator starvation.
  • Extinction Events: With too many foxes or too little food, the ecosystem can collapse entirely. Try to find parameter combinations that sustain both populations.
  • Spatial Refugia Effect: Observe how burrows prevent complete prey extinction, allowing rabbit populations to recover after predator-driven crashes.
  • Nighttime Hunting: Watch fox hunting efficiency change with the day/night cycle, adding another oscillatory layer to population dynamics.
