Ant Colony Food Simulation

Stigmergy-based foraging with dual pheromone trails

What is Ant Colony Foraging?

Ant colonies are one of nature's most remarkable examples of swarm intelligence. Individual ants follow simple local rules — deposit pheromones, follow trails, avoid explored areas — yet the colony as a whole efficiently discovers and exploits food sources across vast territories.

This phenomenon is called stigmergy: indirect coordination through environmental modification. Ants communicate not by talking to each other, but by leaving chemical traces in the environment that influence the behavior of others. This principle has inspired powerful optimization algorithms used in routing, logistics, and network design.

Algorithm Behind the Simulation

This simulation implements a dual-pheromone foraging model:

  • Dual Pheromone System: Two types of chemical signals — food pheromone (orange, deposited when carrying food) and exploration pheromone (cyan, deposited while searching). Food pheromone attracts; exploration pheromone repels (marking already-searched areas).
  • Three-Sensor Antennae: Each ant has left, forward, and right sensors at a configurable distance and angle. The ant steers toward the sensor detecting the strongest food pheromone signal, or away from exploration pheromone.
  • Pheromone Evaporation: Pheromones decay over time (food: ~0.95/frame, exploration: ~0.97/frame), balancing trail maintenance with adaptability to changing conditions.
  • Random Wander: When no pheromone signal is detected, ants perform a random walk, ensuring exploration of unvisited areas.
  • Return-Home Timer: Ants return to the colony after 30 seconds without finding food, preventing indefinite wandering.

Key Equations

Pheromone concentration evolves through deposition and evaporation:

P(t+1) = P(t) × evaporation_rate + deposit_amount

Where evaporation_rate (0.90-0.99) controls how quickly trails fade, and deposit_amount (1-50) determines trail strength.

Ant steering is based on sensor comparison:

turn_direction = argmax(P_left, P_forward, P_right) + wander_noise

Simulation Controls

  • Number of Ants: Colony size (10-500). More ants = faster food discovery but more competition.
  • Ant Speed: Movement speed (0.2-3.0 units/frame).
  • Pheromone Deposit Rate: Trail strength per step (1-50). Higher values create stronger, more persistent trails.
  • Pheromone Evaporation: Retention rate per frame (0.90-0.99). Higher = longer-lasting trails.
  • Wander Strength: Random walk component (0-1). Higher values increase exploration at the cost of trail following.
  • Sensor Angle / Distance: Antenna configuration (10-90 degrees, 5-50 pixels). Controls the ant's "field of view."
  • Food Source Size / Count: Food patch radius (10-80) and number of sources (1-5).

Interesting Behaviors to Observe

  • Trail Emergence: Watch as scattered ants gradually converge on efficient paths between the colony and food sources, forming highways of pheromone.
  • Path Optimization: Over time, shorter paths accumulate more pheromone (more ants complete the trip), naturally selecting for optimal routes.
  • Exploration-Exploitation Balance: The dual pheromone system prevents ants from re-exploring exhausted areas while maintaining scouts for new food sources.
  • Trail Collapse: When a food source is depleted, watch how the trail gradually fades through evaporation and ants redirect to remaining sources.
