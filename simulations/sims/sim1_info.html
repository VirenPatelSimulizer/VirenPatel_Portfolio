Brownian Motion Simulation

Interactive particle dynamics with collision physics

What is Brownian Motion?

Brownian motion is the random, erratic movement of microscopic particles suspended in a fluid (liquid or gas). First observed by botanist Robert Brown in 1827 while studying pollen grains in water, this phenomenon was later explained by Albert Einstein in 1905 as evidence of atomic theory.

The motion occurs because particles are constantly bombarded by molecules of the surrounding medium. Each collision imparts a small momentum change, resulting in the characteristic jittery, zigzag path.

Physics Behind the Simulation

This simulation models several key physical principles:

  • Random Thermal Motion: Particles receive random velocity changes each frame, simulating thermal energy from the surrounding medium. Higher temperature = more energetic motion.
  • Elastic Collisions: When particles collide, momentum and kinetic energy are conserved (with configurable elasticity). The collision response uses proper vector physics.
  • Periodic Boundaries: Particles that exit one edge of the simulation reappear on the opposite side, creating a continuous, unbounded space (toroidal topology).
  • Multi-species Dynamics: Different particle types interact with each other, allowing observation of diffusion and mixing phenomena.

Key Equations

The mean squared displacement of a Brownian particle follows:

⟨x²⟩ = 2Dt

Where D is the diffusion coefficient and t is time. This relationship, derived by Einstein, connects microscopic random motion to macroscopic diffusion.

The diffusion coefficient relates to temperature by the Einstein-Stokes equation:

D = kT / (6πηr)

Where k is Boltzmann's constant, T is temperature, η is fluid viscosity, and r is particle radius.

Simulation Controls

  • Particle Variety: Number of different particle types (1-5). Each type has a distinct color.
  • Type A-E Count: Number of particles for each type. More particles = more collisions and interactions.
  • Temperature: Controls the intensity of random thermal motion. Higher values cause more energetic, faster movement.

Density Visualization

The simulation includes a real-time density plot in the bottom-right corner:

  • 1-2 Particle Types: Shows a linear X-position distribution graph with smooth curves for each type.
  • 3+ Particle Types: Switches to a 2D contour density map showing spatial distribution with color-coded contour lines. Overlapping regions show blended colors indicating diffusion zones.
