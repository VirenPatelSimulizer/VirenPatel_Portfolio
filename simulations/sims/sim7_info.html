Ideal Gas Law Simulation

Kinetic theory of gases with real-time PV=nRT verification

What is the Ideal Gas Law?

The Ideal Gas Law (PV = nRT) is one of the most important equations in physics and chemistry. It relates the macroscopic properties of a gas — pressure (P), volume (V), amount (n), and temperature (T) — through the universal gas constant R.

What makes this simulation remarkable is that PV = nRT is not programmed in — it emerges naturally from simulating individual molecules bouncing around in a container. This demonstrates how macroscopic thermodynamic laws arise from microscopic particle mechanics, a cornerstone of statistical mechanics.

Physics Behind the Simulation

This simulation connects microscopic and macroscopic physics:

  • Kinetic Theory: Temperature is defined as the average kinetic energy of particles: T ∝ ½m⟨v²⟩. Hotter gas = faster particles.
  • Pressure from Collisions: Pressure is measured as the total momentum transferred to the container walls per unit area per unit time. More collisions or faster particles = higher pressure.
  • Elastic Collisions: All particle-particle and particle-wall collisions are perfectly elastic (coefficient of restitution = 1.0), conserving total kinetic energy.
  • Thermostat: A velocity rescaling thermostat gently adjusts particle speeds to maintain the target temperature, simulating thermal contact with a heat reservoir.
  • Free Piston Mode: An optional movable wall adjusts the container volume to maintain constant pressure, demonstrating isobaric processes.

Key Equations

The Ideal Gas Law:

PV = nRT

The simulation verifies this by computing PV/nT in real-time, which should approximate R.

Temperature from kinetic energy (2D):

T = m⟨v²⟩ / (2k_B)

Pressure from wall momentum transfer:

P = Δp / (Δt × A)

Where Δp is the total momentum change from wall collisions, Δt is the measurement interval, and A is the wall area.

The simulation demonstrates the classical gas laws as special cases:

  • Boyle's Law: P ∝ 1/V (at constant T and n)
  • Charles's Law: V ∝ T (at constant P and n)
  • Gay-Lussac's Law: P ∝ T (at constant V and n)
  • Avogadro's Law: V ∝ n (at constant P and T)

Simulation Controls

  • Temperature: Target temperature (100-800 K). Controls average particle kinetic energy.
  • Volume: Container size as percentage (30-100%). Smaller volume = higher pressure at same temperature.
  • Particle Count: Number of gas molecules (50-500). More particles = smoother statistics.
  • Free Piston: Toggle constant-pressure mode. When on, volume adjusts automatically to maintain pressure.
  • Pressure Amplification: Visual scale factor (0.5-20) for the pressure force arrows on walls.
  • Visualization Toggles: Velocity vectors, temperature coloring (blue=slow, red=fast), pressure display (force arrows on walls), and particle trails.

Interesting Experiments to Try

  • Verify Boyle's Law: Keep temperature constant and vary volume. Watch pressure increase inversely with volume in the stats panel.
  • Verify Charles's Law: Enable free piston mode and increase temperature. Watch the volume expand to maintain constant pressure.
  • Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution: Enable temperature coloring to see the distribution of speeds — most particles are moderate, few are very fast or very slow.
  • PV/nT Ratio: Watch the real-time PV/nT calculation in the stats panel. It should stay approximately constant regardless of how you change P, V, n, or T.
  • Particle Trails: Enable trails to visualize the random walk paths of individual molecules, demonstrating the microscopic chaos that produces macroscopic order.
