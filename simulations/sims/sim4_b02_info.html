Layered Aggregates

Bond-based proximity visualization and self-organized structure formation

What are Layered Aggregates?

Layered aggregates form when particles with different interaction properties self-organize into structured arrangements. This simulation is a simplified variant of the linear aggregates model, focusing on bond-based proximity connections between particles to visualize how local interactions produce layered, organized structures.

In materials science, layered self-assembly is fundamental to processes like colloidal crystallization, polymer stratification, and lipid bilayer formation. Understanding how layers emerge from simple particle rules provides insight into designing smart materials.

Physics Behind the Simulation

This simulation models bond-driven self-assembly:

  • Proximity Bonds: Particles within a certain distance form visual bonds, representing the attractive interactions that hold structures together.
  • Self-Propulsion: Particles move actively through the medium, exploring their environment and forming transient connections with neighbors.
  • Short-Range Forces: Linear force falloff between particles creates stable spacing, with attraction at intermediate distances and repulsion at close range.
  • Multi-Species Interactions: Different particle types interact with distinct force profiles, driving spatial segregation and layered organization.

Simulation Controls

  • Particle A/B Count: Number of each particle species (10-500).
  • Radius Scale: Visual size multiplier for particles (1-10).
  • Bond Length Factor: Controls the maximum distance for bond visualization (0-5).
  • Wall Mode: Toggle between periodic boundaries and hard wall bouncing.
  • Spawn Mode: Random or grid-based initial particle placement.

Interesting Behaviors to Observe

  • Network Formation: Watch as isolated particles gradually connect into an interconnected network of bonds.
  • Layer Stratification: Different particle types can separate into distinct spatial layers based on their interaction preferences.
  • Dynamic Restructuring: Bonds constantly form and break as particles move, showing the dynamic equilibrium between thermal motion and bonding forces.
