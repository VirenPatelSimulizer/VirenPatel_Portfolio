Dual Particle Simulation

Interactive dual-species dynamics with configurable inter-particle forces

What is Dual Particle Simulation?

This simulation explores how two distinct populations of particles interact in a shared space. By adjusting the inter-species and intra-species forces, you can observe how simple pairwise interaction rules between particle types A and B lead to complex emergent patterns such as clustering, phase separation, and dynamic equilibria.

The concept draws from research in active matter physics and colloidal science, where mixtures of differently charged or functionalized particles self-organize into surprising structures without any external blueprint.

Physics Behind the Simulation

This simulation models several key physical principles:

  • Inverse-Square Law Forces: Particles interact via forces that scale as 1/r², similar to gravitational and electrostatic interactions. Each pair type (A-A, A-B, B-B) has an independently configurable force strength.
  • Elastic Collisions: When particles overlap, momentum is exchanged with configurable elasticity (0 = perfectly inelastic, 1 = perfectly elastic).
  • Boundary Conditions: Choose between periodic wrap-around (toroidal topology) or hard wall bouncing to study finite-size vs. bulk-like behavior.
  • Bond Length Factor: Controls the equilibrium distance at which attractive forces transition to repulsive, allowing the formation of stable clusters at characteristic spacing.

Key Equations

The pairwise interaction force between particles follows:

F = force_strength / r²

Where force_strength is configurable per pair type (positive = attraction, negative = repulsion) and r is the inter-particle distance. Forces are clamped to a maximum value to prevent numerical instabilities.

Particle dynamics are integrated using the Velocity Verlet method:

x(t+dt) = x(t) + v(t)dt + ½a(t)dt²

Simulation Controls

  • Particle A/B Count: Number of particles for each species (10-500). More particles create richer interaction dynamics.
  • Particle A/B Speed: Initial velocity magnitude (0.1-3.0) for each species.
  • Force A-A / A-B / B-B: Interaction strengths (-10 to 10) for each pair type. Positive values attract, negative values repel.
  • Collision Elasticity: Coefficient of restitution (0-1) controlling energy conservation during collisions.
  • Bond Length Factor: Controls the preferred inter-particle spacing (0-5).
  • Wall Mode: Toggle between periodic boundaries (wrap-around) and hard wall bouncing.

Interesting Behaviors to Observe

  • Phase Separation: Set A-A and B-B attractive, A-B repulsive to watch the two species segregate into distinct clusters, similar to oil and water.
  • Mixing & Diffusion: With all forces attractive, particles form a single mixed cluster, demonstrating miscibility.
  • Predator-Prey Chasing: Set asymmetric A-B forces (one attractive, one repulsive) to create dynamic chasing patterns where one species pursues the other.
  • Crystal-like Ordering: With balanced attraction and repulsion at the right bond length, particles can form ordered lattice-like structures.
